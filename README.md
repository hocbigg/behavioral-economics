## Principles of Microeconomics

The standard model of rational choice, utility maximization, supply and demand, and market equilibrium — the framework that behavioral economics critiques and extends.

[14.01SC Principles of Microeconomics (MIT OpenCourseWare)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-01sc-principles-of-microeconomics-fall-2011) - A complete undergraduate microeconomics course taught by Jon Gruber, with full video lectures, recitation videos, problem sets, and exams. Designed specifically for independent learners in OCW's "Scholar" format. This is the most thorough free option and your best starting point if you want a structured, university-level treatment.

[The Economy 2.0: Microeconomics (CORE Econ)](https://books.core-econ.org/the-economy/microeconomics/0-3-contents.html) - A free, open-access, browser-native textbook that takes a more empirical and policy-oriented approach than traditional texts. It introduces bounded rationality and behavioral considerations early, which makes it a natural bridge into behavioral economics. Use this instead of (or alongside) MIT OCW if you prefer reading to video lectures, or want a more modern, real-world framing.

[Khan Academy Microeconomics (Khan Academy)](https://www.khanacademy.org/economics-finance-domain/microeconomics) - Short, bite-sized video lessons covering all the standard micro topics with built-in practice quizzes. Best as a quick refresher or if you find MIT and CORE too dense at first; it will not substitute for the depth of the other two, but it is a gentler on-ramp.

## Cognitive Psychology and Judgment

The psychological foundations of behavioral economics: heuristics, biases, framing, overconfidence, and the two-system (dual-process) model of thinking.

[PSYC 110: Introduction to Psychology (Open Yale Courses / Paul Bloom)](https://oyc.yale.edu/introduction-psychology/psyc-110) - A complete Yale undergraduate course in general psychology, freely available as video lectures with transcripts. Watch lectures 1–8 and 15–16 in particular (on the brain, perception, memory, decision-making, and social psychology); the full course is worthwhile but those sessions are most directly relevant to behavioral economics. This gives you the psychological vocabulary before you encounter it applied in economic contexts.

[Psychology 2e (OpenStax)](https://openstax.org/details/books/psychology-2e) - A free, peer-reviewed, openly licensed textbook covering introductory psychology. Read the chapters on cognition, memory, judgment, and social psychology. Use this as a reference to deepen what Bloom covers, or as your primary text if you prefer reading to lectures.

[Thinking, Fast and Slow (Daniel Kahneman)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780374533557) - Kahneman's magnum opus summarizing his life's research on judgment and decision-making. It is the single most-cited book in behavioral economics and will profoundly deepen your understanding of the psychological mechanisms. However, it is long and dense — read it alongside (not instead of) the free courses above, and treat it as a slow companion rather than an introduction. The free Open Yale course covers the same intellectual territory at a more accessible pace.

## Behavioral Economics: Core Theory

The systematic study of how human behavior deviates from the rational-choice model: prospect theory, loss aversion, reference dependence, framing effects, time-inconsistent preferences, and social preferences.

[14.13 Psychology and Economics (MIT OpenCourseWare)](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/14-13-psychology-and-economics-spring-2020) - A full MIT undergraduate course on behavioral economics with complete video lectures, slides, and assignments. It covers time preferences, risk preferences, reference-dependent preferences, social preferences, and limited attention — essentially the entire core of the discipline. This is the single most comprehensive free course on behavioral economics available and should anchor your study of the subject.

[Misbehaving: The Making of Behavioral Economics (Richard H. Thaler)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780393352795) - Thaler's narrative history of the field's development, told from his perspective as one of its founders. It is less technical than the MIT course but provides essential context on *why* the field evolved the way it did. Read it alongside the MIT lectures as a narrative companion, not as a substitute for the structured content.

[Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision under Risk (Kahneman & Tversky, 1979)](https://web.mit.edu/curhan/www/docs/Articles/15341_Readings/Behavioral_Decision_Theory/Kahneman_Tversky_1979_Prospect_theory.pdf) - The original 1979 paper that launched modern behavioral economics, freely available as a PDF. It is short (29 pages) and highly readable. Reading this primary source will give you a direct connection to the foundational work that the textbooks describe. "Judgment under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases" (Tversky & Kahneman, *Science*, 1974) is the other seminal paper from the same collaboration — search for the title on Google Scholar to find a free PDF.

[Predictably Irrational (Dan Ariely)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780061353246) - A lighter, more popular treatment of behavioral economics focusing on relatable experiments and everyday irrationality. Read this if you want an accessible entry point before tackling the MIT course, or as an enjoyable parallel read; it is less rigorous but excellent for building intuition.

## Applied Behavioral Economics and Choice Architecture

How behavioral findings are translated into real-world interventions: nudges, defaults, framing in policy, and the design of choice environments.

[Nudge: Improving Decisions about Health, Wealth, and Happiness (Thaler & Sunstein)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780300122237) - The canonical text on choice architecture and libertarian paternalism. It defines the vocabulary (nudges, defaults, choice architecture) that is now standard in policy and business applications. This book is essential if you want to understand how behavioral economics is actually used in practice. There is no adequate free equivalent, so this paid book is justified.

[Behavioral Economics: Past, Present, and Future (Richard H. Thaler, American Economic Review, 2016)](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.106.7.1577) - Thaler's Nobel-lecture-style review article, freely downloadable as a PDF from the American Economic Association. It surveys the entire field and its applications in about 20 pages, making it a concise free supplement that covers much of the same ground as *Nudge* at a more academic level. Read this alongside *Nudge* — the article provides the intellectual framework while the book provides the extended examples.

[A Beginner's Guide to Irrational Behavior (Dan Ariely / Duke University)](https://danariely.com/coursera) - The archived course materials from Ariely's popular Coursera MOOC, hosted on his personal site. The videos and readings cover applications of behavioral economics to money, work, dishonesty, and self-control. The course itself is no longer active on Coursera, but the archived materials remain freely accessible and useful for their applied perspective.
